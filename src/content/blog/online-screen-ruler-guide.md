## How Screen Measurement Works

A screen ruler does not magically know the physical size of your display. It works by:

1.  **Detecting your screen's DPI** (dots per inch) — the number of physical pixels per inch of your actual screen.
2.  **Mapping pixel counts to physical units.** If your screen is 96 DPI, then 96 pixels = 1 inch = 25.4 mm.
3.  **Drawing tick marks** at intervals that correspond to real-world measurements at your screen's DPI.

The challenge: DPI varies between devices. A laptop might be 96 DPI, a phone might be 300+ DPI, and a 4K monitor might be 163 DPI. Using the wrong DPI drastically throws off measurements.

## Calibrating for Perfect Accuracy

If the default DPI does not match your screen, calibration is essential. Our ruler includes a calibration tool that uses objects with known physical sizes:

### Calibration Objects That Work Perfectly

| Object | Exact Size | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| Credit/Debit Card | 85.6 mm × 54 mm (3.37 in wide) | Universal — everyone has one |
| US Quarter | 24.26 mm diameter | Small screen calibration |
| A4 Paper | 210 mm wide | Large screen calibration |
| Standard Postage Stamp | Varies by country | Approximate calibration |

### How to Calibrate

1.  Place the physical object (like a credit card) against your screen.
2.  Drag the calibration slider until the on-screen ruler matches the object's known width.
3.  Your calibration is saved in browser storage. You will not need to recalibrate unless you switch devices.

Once calibrated, the ruler becomes accurate to within approximately ±1 mm — matching a physical ruler.

The ruler instantly switches between four units:

*   **Millimeters (mm):** Best for precision work — small parts, jewelry, and electronics.
*   **Inches (in):** Standard in the United States for construction, crafts, and DIY.
*   **Pixels (px):** Useful for designers and developers checking element sizes or screen layouts.

## Common Measuring Scenarios

### Measure a Photo for a Frame

Place the photo against your screen, calibrate the ruler with a credit card, and measure width and height. You will know exactly what frame size to buy.

### Check Jewelry or Component Size Online

If a product listing says "25 mm diameter," switch the ruler to mm mode and see exactly how large that is on your screen. This reduces returns from misjudged sizes.

### Measure Small DIY Parts

Screws, bolts, washers — calibrate once and measure quickly without hunting for your toolbox ruler.

### Size Wire or Cable Thickness

Lay the cable across the screen (or hold it against it) and check its thickness in mm. Useful for identifying gauge when ordering replacement parts.
