## The Three Most Common Percentage Calculations

### 1. Finding a Percentage of a Number

The formula: **Result = Number × (Percentage ÷ 100)**. Example: What is 25% of 80? 80 × 0.25 = 20. This is the most common use case — calculating discounts, tips, taxes, and commissions.

### 2. Finding What Percentage One Number Is of Another

The formula: **Percentage = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100**. Example: If you scored 42 out of 50 on a test, what is your percentage? (42 ÷ 50) × 100 = 84%. This is used for test scores, survey results, and budget tracking.

### 3. Calculating Percentage Increase or Decrease

The formula: **% Change = ((New − Old) ÷ Old) × 100**. Example: A stock price rises from $50 to $65. (65 − 50) ÷ 50 × 100 = 30% increase. Negative results indicate a decrease. This is essential for investment tracking, sales performance, and inflation calculations.

## The Discount Calculator: Never Overpay Again

When you see "30% off" during a sale, your actual price is: **Sale Price = Original Price × (1 − Discount Percentage ÷ 100)**. A $120 item at 30% off costs $120 × 0.70 = $84. But be careful — retailers sometimes raise the original price before applying a "discount." Compare prices across stores before buying.
