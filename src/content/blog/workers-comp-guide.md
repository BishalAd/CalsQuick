Workers compensation is a state-mandated insurance program that provides wage replacement and medical benefits to employees injured on the job. If you have been hurt at work, understanding how your benefits are calculated is the first step to making sure you receive what you are entitled to. Our [Workers Comp Calculator](/workers-comp-calculator) estimates your weekly benefits, total payments, and potential settlement value for all 50 states.

## How Weekly Benefits Are Calculated

The standard formula in most states: Weekly Benefit = Average Weekly Wage × Replacement Rate, capped at the state maximum. Your AWW is typically your total gross wages in the 52 weeks before injury divided by 52. Most states replace two-thirds (66.67%) of your AWW. A worker earning $1,200/week in California receives approximately $800/week, limited to the state maximum of $1,620/week.

## Four Types of Disability Benefits

Temporary Total Disability (TTD) pays when you are completely off work recovering. Temporary Partial Disability (TPD) covers the wage gap when you return to light duty at reduced pay. Permanent Partial Disability (PPD) is a lump sum or weekly payment based on a doctor's impairment rating after you reach Maximum Medical Improvement. Permanent Total Disability (PTD) provides ongoing benefits — sometimes for life — if you cannot return to any work.

## What Your Settlement Might Be Worth

Settlements typically range from 1× to 3× the total benefit value, heavily influenced by future medical costs, your impairment rating, and whether you have legal representation. Workers comp attorneys work on contingency (no upfront cost) and typically recover 15-25% more than unrepresented workers.
