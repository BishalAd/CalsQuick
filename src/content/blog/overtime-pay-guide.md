Under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), non-exempt employees must receive overtime pay at a rate of at least 1.5× their regular rate for all hours worked beyond 40 in a workweek. This "time and a half" rule covers approximately 80% of US workers. ## Federal vs State Overtime Rules

The FLSA sets the floor — states can require more. California has the strictest rules: 1.5× over 8 hours/day, double time (2×) over 12 hours/day, plus 1.5× for the first 8 hours on the 7th consecutive workday. Alaska and Nevada require 1.5× over 8 hours/day. Colorado requires 1.5× over 12 hours/day. When both daily and weekly overtime apply, the employee receives whichever rate is higher.

## Overtime for Different Pay Types

Hourly workers: overtime rate = 1.5 × hourly rate. Salaried non-exempt workers: first convert to hourly by dividing annual salary by 2080 hours, then apply 1.5×. Tipped employees: overtime is based on the full minimum wage, not just the cash wage. Multi-rate workers: use the blended (weighted average) rate of all jobs worked for the same employer in that week.

## Common Overtime Violations

Employers cannot average hours across two weeks to avoid overtime, ask workers to clock out and keep working, give comp time instead of pay in the private sector, or misclassify employees as exempt to avoid overtime. Violations can be reported to the Department of Labor Wage and Hour Division.
