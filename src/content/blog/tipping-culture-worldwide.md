Tipping customs vary dramatically around the world — what is considered generous in one country is insulting in another. In Japan, tipping can be seen as offensive. In the United States, not tipping a restaurant server is a social faux pas. In Iceland, there is no tipping expectation at all. This guide covers tipping norms for over 20 countries so you always know what to leave — and when to leave nothing at all.

## Tipping in the United States

The US has the most complex and socially enforced tipping culture in the world, largely because service industry workers receive a **tipped minimum wage** of $2.13/hour (federal rate), relying on tips for the majority of their income.

**Standard US tip rates:**

| Service | Expected Tip |
|---|---|
| Sit-down restaurant | 15% (poor service) – 20% (standard) – 25% (excellent) |
| Food delivery | $3–$5 minimum, or 15–20% of order |
| Coffee shop / counter service | $1–$2, or 10% (discretionary) |
| Bartender | $1–$2 per drink, or 15–20% of tab |
| Hotel housekeeping | $2–$5 per night |
| Hotel bellhop / valet | $2–$5 per bag |
| Taxi / rideshare | 10–20% |
| Hair salon / barber | 15–20% |
| Nail salon | 15–20% |
| Spa / massage | 15–20% |
| Airport / hotel shuttle | $1–$2 per person |

**Important:** Tip on the **pre-tax** amount. You should not tip on the sales tax portion of your bill.

**Calculating a US restaurant tip:**
- Bill before tax: $72.00
- 20% tip: $72 × 0.20 = **$14.40**
- Total: $86.40

## Tipping in Canada

Similar to the US, tipping is standard in Canada. The Canadian tipped minimum wage varies by province (typically $10–$13/hour), making tips an important part of service worker income.

- Restaurants: **15% minimum, 18–20% standard**
- Taxis: **10–15%**
- Bars: **$1–$2/drink**
- Hotels: **$2–$3 per bag**, $1–$3/night housekeeping

## Tipping in the United Kingdom

Tipping in the UK is expected in restaurants but less enforced than in North America. Service staff are paid the full national minimum wage (£11.44/hour as of 2024), reducing dependency on tips.

- Restaurants: **10–15%** — but check if a "service charge" is already included (many London restaurants add 12.5%)
- Pubs: Tipping at the bar is uncommon; occasionally leaving "one for yourself" is a friendly gesture
- Taxis: **10%** or round up to the nearest pound
- Hotel porters: **£1–£2 per bag**

**Note:** If a service charge is already on the bill, you are not obligated to tip further.

## Tipping in Europe

European tipping expectations are generally much lower than North America, since workers are paid full legal wages.

| Country | Restaurants | Taxis | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| France | 5–10% (optional) | Round up | Service charge often included |
| Germany | 5–10% | Round up | Say "stimmt so" to keep the change |
| Italy | €1–€2 (optional) | Round up | Coperto (cover charge) is common |
| Spain | 5–10% (optional) | Round up | Less expected in casual dining |
| Netherlands | 5–10% | 10% | Increasingly expected in cities |
| Switzerland | Not required | Not required | Service included in prices |
| Sweden | Not expected | Not expected | All workers paid full wages |
| Norway | Not expected | Not expected | Same as Sweden |
| Iceland | Not expected | Not expected | Considered unnecessary |

## Tipping in Asia

**Japan** — Tipping is considered **rude and offensive** in Japan. Excellent service is a professional standard, not something that warrants extra payment. Never tip in restaurants, hotels, or taxis.

**South Korea** — Tipping is generally not practiced and can cause awkwardness. High-end international hotels may be more accepting.

**China** — Tipping is not customary in mainland China. In Hong Kong, a 10% service charge is often added to the bill, and no additional tip is needed.

**India** — Tipping is appreciated and expected in tourist areas.
- Restaurants: **10% of the bill**
- Hotels: ₹20–₹50 for porters
- Taxis: Round up or 10%
- Tour guides: ₹100–₹500/day depending on tour length

**Thailand** — Tipping is appreciated:
- Restaurants: ฿20–฿50 for casual, 10% in nicer restaurants
- Massage therapists: ฿50–฿100
- Taxis: Round up

**Singapore** — A 10% service charge is automatically added to most bills. Additional tipping is not expected but appreciated.

## Tipping in Australia and New Zealand

**Australia** — Tipping is not mandatory; all workers receive a full wage under Australian law. However, tipping is becoming more common in cities and at upscale venues.
- Restaurants: 10% at nicer establishments, nothing at casual cafés
- Taxis: Round up (optional)

**New Zealand** — Similar to Australia. Tipping is uncommon and not expected. Excellent service is the professional standard.

## Tipping in the Middle East

**UAE (Dubai, Abu Dhabi)** — 10–15% in restaurants; hotel service charges (10%) often included. Tipping is appreciated.

**Saudi Arabia** — 10% in restaurants if service charge not included.

**Israel** — 10–15% in restaurants is standard. Check if service is included.
