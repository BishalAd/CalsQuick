Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) are mandatory annual withdrawals from tax-deferred retirement accounts — Traditional IRAs, 401(k)s, 403(b)s, SEP IRAs, and similar plans. The IRS requires these withdrawals to ensure that tax-deferred savings eventually generate tax revenue. Missing an RMD triggers a penalty of 25% of the missed amount (reduced from 50% by SECURE 2.0). ## New RMD Starting Ages Under SECURE 2.0

Born 1951-1959: RMDs start at age 73. Born 1960 or later: RMDs start at age 75. Roth 401(k)s no longer require RMDs starting in 2024 — a major change. The first RMD can be delayed until April 1 of the following year, but this means taking two RMDs in one year, which may push you into a higher tax bracket.

## How RMDs Are Calculated

The formula: RMD = Account Balance (December 31 of prior year) ÷ IRS Life Expectancy Factor. For age 73, the factor is 26.5 — meaning you must withdraw approximately 3.77% of your balance. By age 85, the factor drops to 16.0 (6.25% withdrawal rate). The IRS Uniform Lifetime Table (updated 2022) provides factors for ages 72 through 120+.

## Tax-Smart RMD Strategies

Qualified Charitable Distributions (QCDs) let you donate up to $105,000 directly from an IRA to charity — counting toward your RMD but excluded from taxable income. Roth conversions before RMD age reduce future RMDs by shifting funds to tax-free accounts. For multiple IRAs, calculate each account's RMD separately but withdraw the total from any combination of IRA accounts.
