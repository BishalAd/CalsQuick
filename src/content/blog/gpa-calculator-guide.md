Your Grade Point Average (GPA) is the single number that summarizes your entire academic record. Colleges evaluate it for admissions, scholarship committees depend on it for awards, and some employers even check it for internships. Understanding exactly how GPA is calculated — and how to improve it strategically — can make a significant difference in your academic trajectory. ## How GPA Is Calculated

GPA = (Sum of Grade Points × Credit Hours) ÷ Total Credit Hours. The standard 4.0 scale:

| Letter Grade | Grade Points |
|---|---|
| A+ / A | 4.0 |
| A− | 3.7 |
| B+ | 3.3 |
| B | 3.0 |
| B− | 2.7 |
| C+ | 2.3 |
| C | 2.0 |
| C− | 1.7 |
| D+ | 1.3 |
| D | 1.0 |
| F | 0.0 |

**Example:** If you take a 3-credit course and earn a B+, you receive 3.3 × 3 = 9.9 grade points. Total grade points across all courses divided by total credits equals your GPA.

## Weighted vs. Unweighted GPA

- **Unweighted GPA:** Standard 4.0 scale. An A in regular English and an A in AP Calculus both count as 4.0. This is the most common measure.
- **Weighted GPA:** AP, IB, and Honors courses earn bonus points (typically +1.0, making A = 5.0). This rewards students who challenge themselves academically.

Top colleges look at both — a 3.8 unweighted with rigorous coursework is more impressive than a 4.0 with easy classes.

## What Is a Good GPA?

| GPA Range | Admissions Outlook |
|---|---|
| 3.8–4.0 | Competitive for Ivy League and top-tier universities |
| 3.5–3.8 | Strong for competitive state schools |
| 3.0–3.5 | Solid for regional universities |
| 2.5–3.0 | Admissible to many schools; below average for competitive programs |
| Below 2.0 | Academic probation risk; retake courses to improve |

## How to Strategically Raise Your GPA

1. **Prioritize high-credit courses.** An A in a 4-credit chemistry lab impacts your GPA four times more than an A in a 1-credit seminar.
2. **Retake low grades.** Many colleges replace the old grade when you retake a course, instantly boosting your GPA.
3. **Use the target GPA tool.** Enter your current GPA, desired target, and remaining credits to see exactly what average you need.
4. **Focus on consistent improvement.** Even a 2.5 after freshman year can become a 3.2+ with four semesters of 3.5+ performance.
