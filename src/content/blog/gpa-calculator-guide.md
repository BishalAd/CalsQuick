## How GPA is Calculated: The Core Formula

A Grade Point Average is a **weighted average** based on course credits (or hours). This means that a course with more credits has a larger impact on your GPA than a course with fewer credits.

The calculation follows a simple three-step process:
1. Convert your letter grade in each course to its numerical value (grade points).
2. Multiply each course's grade points by the number of credit hours for that course. This gives you the total **Quality Points** for the class.
3. Divide the sum of all Quality Points by the total number of credit hours completed.

### The Formula:

$$\text{GPA} = \frac{\sum (\text{Grade Points} \times \text{Credit Hours})}{\text{Total Credit Hours}}$$

## The Standard 4.0 Letter Grade Scale

To calculate your GPA, convert your letter grades to grade points using the standard 4.0 scale below:

| Letter Grade | Percentage Equivalent | Standard 4.0 Grade Points |
|---|---|---|
| **A+ / A** | 93–100% | **4.0** |
| **A−** | 90–92% | **3.7** |
| **B+** | 87–89% | **3.3** |
| **B** | 83–86% | **3.0** |
| **B−** | 80–82% | **2.7** |
| **C+** | 77–79% | **2.3** |
| **C** | 73–76% | **2.0** |
| **C−** | 70–72% | **1.7** |
| **D+** | 67–69% | **1.3** |
| **D** | 65–66% | **1.0** |
| **F** | Below 65% | **0.0** |

## Worked Example: Calculating a Semester GPA

Consider a student with the following semester grades:

- **English 101:** A (4.0 grade points) — 3 credits
- **Calculus I:** B+ (3.3 grade points) — 4 credits
- **Chemistry Lab:** A- (3.7 grade points) — 1 credit
- **History 201:** C (2.0 grade points) — 3 credits

### Step 1: Calculate Quality Points for each class
- **English 101:** $4.0 \times 3 = 12.0$ Quality Points
- **Calculus I:** $3.3 \times 4 = 13.2$ Quality Points
- **Chemistry Lab:** $3.7 \times 1 = 3.7$ Quality Points
- **History 201:** $2.0 \times 3 = 6.0$ Quality Points

### Step 2: Sum the Quality Points and Credits
- **Total Quality Points:** $12.0 + 13.2 + 3.7 + 6.0 = 34.9$
- **Total Credits:** $3 + 4 + 1 + 3 = 11$ credits

### Step 3: Divide Quality Points by Credits
- **GPA:** $34.9 \div 11 = \mathbf{3.17}$

## Weighted vs. Unweighted GPA: What's the Difference?

High schools and colleges treat advanced classes differently, leading to two types of GPA scores:

### 1. Unweighted GPA
An unweighted GPA is calculated on the standard 4.0 scale, regardless of course difficulty. An "A" in AP Physics counts as a 4.0, and an "A" in physical education counts as a 4.0. The maximum possible unweighted GPA is 4.0.

### 2. Weighted GPA
A weighted GPA rewards students for taking more challenging courses. It adds extra points (usually 0.5 to 1.0) to the grade point value of Honors, Advanced Placement (AP), or International Baccalaureate (IB) courses.

- **AP/IB Courses:** Usually receive a **1.0 point bump** (A = 5.0, B = 4.0, C = 3.0).
- **Honors Courses:** Usually receive a **0.5 point bump** (A = 4.5, B = 3.5, C = 2.5).

A student who gets an "A" in an AP course earns a 5.0 grade point, allowing their weighted GPA to rise above 4.0 (often up to 4.5 or 5.0 depending on the school's policy).

**Which matters more?**  
Admissions committees at competitive colleges look at both. A 3.8 unweighted GPA in a schedule full of AP and Honors classes is generally more impressive than a 4.0 unweighted GPA in standard, less challenging classes.

## Strategic Tips to Raise Your Cumulative GPA

If your GPA is lower than you want, raising it takes mathematical strategy:

**1. Focus on high-credit courses.**  
Because GPA is a weighted average, an "A" in a 4-credit course has four times the positive impact of an "A" in a 1-credit course. Focus your study efforts on classes that carry the most credit hours.

**2. Retake courses with failing or D grades.**  
Many schools offer "grade replacement" policies. If you retake a course you did poorly in, your new grade replaces the old grade in your cumulative GPA calculation, removing the low grade from your record. Replacing an "F" (0.0) with an "A" (4.0) yields an immediate, massive bump in your cumulative GPA.

**3. Protect your GPA with Pass/Fail options.**  
If your school allows you to take elective courses on a Pass/Fail (or Credit/No Credit) basis, use it for classes that are outside your comfort zone. Passing the course gives you credit toward graduation without affecting your GPA if you get a lower grade.

**4. Set a Target GPA and plan backward.**  
Determine your goal GPA and calculate the average grades you need in your remaining semesters. If you are a junior with a 2.8 GPA and want to graduate with a 3.0, you must maintain a higher average in your remaining classes to pull the cumulative average up.
