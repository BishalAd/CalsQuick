## What Is EMI?

EMI stands for **Equated Monthly Installment** — the fixed amount you pay to your lender every month to repay a loan. Each EMI has two components:

*   **Principal Portion:** The part that goes toward repaying the actual loan amount. This portion increases every month.
*   **Interest Portion:** The cost the lender charges on the outstanding balance. This portion is highest in the first month and decreases over time.

In the early months of a loan, most of your EMI goes toward interest. By the final months, almost all of it goes toward principal. This is why prepaying early saves the most interest.

---

## The EMI Formula Explained

The standard EMI formula (reducing balance method) is:

> EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n − 1)

Where:
*   **P** = Principal loan amount
*   **r** = Monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)
*   **n** = Total number of monthly installments (loan tenure in years × 12)

**Example:** A Rs. 50 lakh (5,000,000) home loan at 10.5% for 20 years (240 months):
*   r = 10.5 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.00875
*   EMI = 5,000,000 × 0.00875 × (1.00875)^240 / ((1.00875)^240 − 1)
*   **EMI = Rs. 49,919 per month**
*   Total repayment = Rs. 1,19,80,560 — of which Rs. 69,80,560 is interest

## Flat Rate vs. Reducing Balance Interest

This distinction is critical — it can mean the difference of lakhs of rupees in total interest paid:

### Reducing Balance (Diminishing Balance) Method

Interest is calculated only on the **outstanding principal** each month. As you repay, the principal decreases, so the interest charged each month also decreases. This is the fair, standard method used by most banks for home loans, car loans, and personal loans.

### Flat Rate Method

Interest is calculated on the **original loan amount** for the entire tenure — regardless of how much you have already repaid. The effective interest rate is nearly double the advertised flat rate.

**Comparison on a Rs. 5 lakh, 3-year loan at "10%" rate:**

| Method | Monthly EMI | Total Interest | Effective Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reducing Balance | Rs. 16,134 | Rs. 80,816 | 10.0% |
| Flat Rate | Rs. 18,056 | Rs. 1,50,000 | ~17.9% |

Always confirm with your lender that they use the reducing balance method. Our calculator uses the standard reducing balance formula.

*   Starting balance

## How to Reduce Your EMI or Save on Interest

### 1. Increase the Loan Tenure

Longer tenure = lower monthly EMI (but more total interest). A 30-year home loan has a lower EMI than a 20-year one, but you pay significantly more interest over the life of the loan.

### 2. Make a Larger Down Payment

Borrowing less reduces both your EMI and total interest. On a Rs. 50 lakh home at 10.5% for 20 years, increasing your down payment from 20% to 30% saves approximately Rs. 6,000/month in EMI.

### 3. Negotiate a Lower Interest Rate

Even a 0.5% rate reduction saves significant money. On a Rs. 50 lakh, 20-year loan, reducing the rate from 10.5% to 10% saves Rs. 18,000 per year in interest.

### 4. Prepay the Loan

Making extra payments directly toward the principal reduces future interest. A single extra EMI payment per year on a 20-year home loan can reduce the tenure by 4–6 years and save lakhs in interest. Some banks charge a prepayment penalty (typically 1–2% in Nepal).

## Loan Eligibility and Rates in Nepal

### Home Loans

*   **Interest rates:** 9–12% per annum (varies by bank)
*   **Loan-to-Value ratio:** Banks typically lend 60–70% of property value
*   **EMI-to-Income ratio:** Your EMI should not exceed 40–50% of monthly income
*   **Tenure:** Up to 25 years depending on age
*   **Processing fee:** 0.5–1.5% of loan amount

### Major Banks and Typical Rates

Rates are linked to the Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB) base rate and change periodically. Check with individual banks like NMB Bank, Everest Bank, Standard Chartered Nepal, NIC Asia Bank, and Global IME Bank for current home and auto loan rates.

### Required Documents

*   Citizenship certificate or passport
*   Income proof (salary slips, tax returns, audited financials for self-employed)
*   Property documents (for home loans)
*   Bank statements (typically 6–12 months)
*   Employment verification letter
