An annuity is a contract with an insurance company designed to provide steady income — typically during retirement. You pay a lump sum or series of payments, and in return, the insurer guarantees regular payments to you for life or a specified period. Our [Annuity Calculator](/annuity-calculator) lets you model present value, future value, and payment amounts instantly.

## How an Annuity Works: The Two Phases

Every annuity has an accumulation phase and a payout phase. During accumulation, your money grows tax-deferred. During payout (annuitization), the insurer converts your balance into a stream of payments. The key decision: when do you need the income? Immediate annuities begin paying within 12 months. Deferred annuities let your money grow for years before payouts start.

## Fixed, Variable, and Indexed: Choosing Your Risk Level

Fixed annuities offer guaranteed interest rates (3-6% as of 2025). Variable annuities invest in market sub-accounts — higher potential returns but no principal guarantee. Fixed indexed annuities tie returns to a market index like the S&P 500 with downside protection, giving you some growth without losing money in down markets.

## The Annuity Formula: Understanding the Math

Present Value: PV = PMT × [1 − (1 + r)^(−n)] / r. This tells you the lump sum needed today to generate specific payments. Future Value: FV = PMT × [(1 + r)^n − 1] / r. This projects what regular contributions grow to. For annuity due (payments at start of period), multiply by (1 + r) — these are worth more because each payment earns an extra period of interest.

## Payout Options Matter Immensely

Life-only pays the highest monthly amount but stops at death. Joint-and-survivor continues for your spouse. Period-certain guarantees payments for a set number of years. Cash refund returns unpaid principal to heirs. These choices are irreversible, so model them carefully. Our calculator lets you test different scenarios before committing.
