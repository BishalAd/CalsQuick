A 401(k) is the most powerful retirement savings vehicle available to American workers. Over 70 million Americans participate, with total plan assets exceeding $7 trillion. The combination of tax-deferred growth, high contribution limits, and employer matching makes it unmatched for building retirement wealth. ## 2024-2025 Contribution Limits

For 2024: $23,000 under age 50, $30,500 for age 50+ (includes $7,500 catch-up). For 2025: $23,500 under 50, $31,000 for 50+. Total plan contributions (employee + employer) cannot exceed $69,000 (2024) or $70,000 (2025).

## Employer Match: Never Leave Free Money on the Table

The most common formula is 50% match on the first 6% of salary. On a $60,000 salary contributing 6% ($3,600), your employer adds $1,800. That is an immediate, guaranteed 50% return. Over 30 years at 7%, that $1,800/year match alone grows to approximately $170,000. Contributing less than the match threshold is literally turning down free money.

## The Starting Age Math: Why 10 Years Changes Everything

A 25-year-old contributing $500/month at 7% reaches $1.2 million by 65. A 35-year-old starting the same contribution reaches $567,000. The 10-year delay costs $633,000. Time in the market beats timing the market — every single time.

## Traditional vs Roth 401(k)

Traditional contributions reduce your taxable income now, but withdrawals are taxed in retirement. Roth contributions give no upfront tax break, but qualified withdrawals are completely tax-free. Young savers in the 12-22% brackets often benefit more from Roth. High earners in 32%+ brackets usually benefit from Traditional. Many advisors recommend splitting contributions between both.

## Never Cash Out Early

Withdrawing before 59½ triggers a 10% penalty plus ordinary income tax — typically 30-40% total. A $50,000 withdrawal could net only $33,000. Plus, that money loses decades of compound growth. The true cost is often $200,000+ in lost retirement savings. Roll over, don't cash out.
