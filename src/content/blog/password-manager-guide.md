# Why You Need a Password Manager

In today’s digital world, the average person manages dozens of online accounts. Reusing passwords across sites creates a single point of failure: if one service is compromised, attackers can try the same credentials elsewhere. A password manager solves this by generating and storing strong, unique passwords for each account, protected by a single master key.

# How an Offline Password Manager Works

Unlike cloud‑based vaults that store your data on remote servers, an **offline password manager** performs all encryption and storage locally in your browser. Your passwords are encrypted with a key derived from your master password and never transmitted to any server. Even if someone gains access to your device’s storage, decrypting your passwords without the master key is computationally infeasible.

# Best Practices for Password Security

1. **Use a strong master password**: Choose a passphrase of at least 12–16 characters that combines words, numbers, and symbols.
2. **Enable two‑factor authentication (2FA)**: Wherever possible, add a second layer of security to your accounts.
3. **Regularly audit your passwords**: Identify and change any reused, weak, or compromised passwords.
4. **Keep your device secure**: Use device encryption, strong login passwords, and updated software to protect against malware.

# Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is my data safe with this password manager?**  
A: Yes. The tool performs all operations locally in your browser. No data is sent to any server, and your passwords never leave your device.

**Q: Can I generate strong passwords?**  
A: Yes, the tool includes a strong password generator that creates random, high‑entropy passwords based on your length and complexity preferences.

**Q: What happens if I forget my master password?**  
A: Since the tool stores data only locally and does not transmit it, there is no recovery mechanism. If you forget your master password, you will lose access to stored passwords.
